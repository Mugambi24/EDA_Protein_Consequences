Phased-allele EDA isoform RNA deconvolution (synthetic benchmarking assay)

Scientific objective. Quantify whether the phased variant-bearing allele (hap1) has a different A1/A2 splice-product balance from the other allele (hap0), and whether that allelic contrast changes between case and control. Each library batch has different barcode/junction misassignment and RNA-capture efficiency; the same read class can arise from more than one latent product. Neither raw A1 fractions nor simple pooled unique reads reliably recover the allele-specific biological effect. The simulation is not a clinical sequencing study.

Input. The CLI option `--phased-assay ABSOLUTE_PATH` supplies JSON with exactly these top-level keys: `assay_id` (string), `synthetic` (boolean, true), `signal_categories` (array), `product_classes` (array), `standards` (array), `measurements` (array). The four `product_classes`, in this order, are `hap0_A1`, `hap0_A2`, `hap1_A1`, `hap1_A2`. The nine observed `signal_categories`, in this order, are `h0_A1`, `h0_A2`, `h1_A1`, `h1_A2`, `h0_shared`, `h1_shared`, `unphased_A1`, `unphased_A2`, `unassigned`. `h0`/`h1` denote observed haplotype tags, not perfectly measured truth. `shared` lacks an informative splice-junction tag; `unphased` lacks a reliable allele tag. Every category can contribute to statistical inference, but `unassigned` is excluded from the informative-molecule total.

Each standard has exactly `batch_id`, `standard_id`, `replicate_id`, `known_product_fractions`, `counts`. The fractions object has exactly four product keys, nonnegative finite numbers totaling 1.0; counts has exactly nine category keys of nonnegative JSON integers. Pure standards contain one known product at fraction 1. Mixed standards contain at least three known products at positive fraction. Multiple standards and four independent replicates are available in each lane, including one intentionally contaminated mixed aliquot whose actual composition does not match its declared fractions. Estimate batch-specific observed-category emissions and relative product-capture efficiencies from standards; pure standards alone cannot identify relative capture. Identify and limit the influence of contaminated standards without using the ground truth. Distinct statistical methods are acceptable, provided the independent measurements are reconstructed accurately.

Reject repeated calibration-standard `(batch_id,standard_id,replicate_id)` tuples with a nonzero exit; repeated tuples do not represent independent calibration replicates. Each measurement has exactly `case_id`, `group` (`case` or `control`), `batch_id`, `replicate_id`, and `counts` (the same nine-category object). Match case IDs to the DNA/RNA inputs exactly, and reject missing, extra or repeated (case,group,batch,replicate) identifiers with a nonzero exit. Do not assume the ordering of any input arrays. An informative batch has >=150 non-`unassigned` observed molecules and calibrators for all four products plus >=4 distinct mixed calibration compositions (count distinct ordered four-fraction vectors, not standard IDs). Zero-valued fractions are valid in mixed standards with three positive products; these standards must be included as calibration evidence. Each used calibration composition must have at least three replicates. At least two distinct informative calibrated batches per case/group are necessary for estimation, but two valid batches are NOT sufficient when either phased allele remains poorly identified. One batch may be underpowered or omitted. Only molecules in retained batches contribute to `informative_molecules`. Unassigned molecules can inform mixture fitting but do not contribute to this reported count.

Inference. Fit a nonnegative four-product mixture, accounting for each batch's category-mapping errors, unequal product capture and replicate calibration uncertainty. From the corrected four fractions compute the A1 fraction among hap0 products and among hap1 products, separately for case and control. `allelic_contrast` is hap1 A1 fraction minus hap0 A1 fraction. `variant_linked_shift` is case contrast minus control contrast. Report an interval for each haplotype-specific fraction that reflects measurement variance and between-batch disagreement. A reproducible profile likelihood, bootstrap, or analytically justified approximation is acceptable. With less than two informative batches, or when either haplotype is not identifiable from the observations, do not impute a fraction. Operational identifiability rule: if the fitted, capture-corrected total abundance of either haplotype is below 0.07 of the four-product mixture, treat the whole case/group panel as unidentifiable (even if TWO or THREE batches are retained); this guards against reporting a splicing ratio from effectively absent allele evidence. Do not drop valid calibrated batches merely to force an unidentifiable output. If there are fewer than two batches, identifiability fails independently of any fitted abundance. Explicitly use mixed calibrators: pooling raw h0/h1 observed calls, neglecting misassignment, or assuming equal capture will be inaccurate.

Output. The CLI writes `/app/output/phased_rna.json` with exactly `assay_id` and `records`. Records are unique and sorted lexicographically by `case_id`, matching the DNA cases. Each record has exactly `case_id`, `case`, `control`, `variant_linked_shift`, `linkage_class`. Each panel has exactly `hap0_a1_fraction`, `hap1_a1_fraction`, `allelic_contrast`, `hap0_interval_95`, `hap1_interval_95`, `informative_molecules`, `batches_used`, `quality_flag`. The two count fields must be nonnegative JSON integers (not booleans/floats). Estimated fractions and interval bounds are finite in [0,1]; allelic_contrast is finite in [-1,1]; variant_linked_shift is finite in [-2,2] because it is the difference of two allelic contrasts. All of these non-null numeric values are rounded to at most six decimal places. Intervals have exactly two ascending bounds in [0,1] containing their corresponding fraction. Set `quality_flag` to `estimated` only when both haplotype fractions are identifiable. Otherwise use `unidentifiable` with null fractions, contrast and intervals, whether the problem is too few informative batches or inadequate haplotype identifiability despite two or three informative batches. `batches_used` is the actual count of retained, calibrated batches meeting the molecule and calibrator criteria, not an identifiability flag: an unidentifiable panel can therefore have 0, 1, 2 or 3 batches used. `informative_molecules` always counts non-`unassigned` molecules in those retained batches; never lower either count to justify a null estimate. `allelic_contrast` is the rounded hap1-minus-hap0 already rounded fractions; `variant_linked_shift` is case-minus-control rounded contrasts, null if either panel unidentifiable. `linkage_class` is `unidentifiable` for null shift; `hap1_A1_increased` for delta >=0.18; `hap1_A1_decreased` for <=-0.18; otherwise `no_clear_linkage_shift`.

Scientific adequacy on independently generated, withheld synthetic mixtures. For EACH haplotype separately (not a pooled hap0/hap1 mean), mean absolute error must be <=0.04; every error must be <0.09 and all but at most ONE case/group point estimate per fixture must have absolute error <0.08. The single-outlier allowance accommodates stochastic calibration noise without allowing repeated large deviations. At least 85% of that haplotype's identifiable reference fractions must lie within their unexpanded reported intervals. For EACH haplotype separately, mean interval width must be <0.28 and maximum width <0.45. Intervals must vary with evidence across case/group/haplotype panels; identical universal fixed bounds do not express measurement uncertainty. Each graded sealed case must increase its case hap1 A1 fraction by more than 0.03 when up to 80 hap1_A2 molecules per case-library are reassigned to hap1_A1; calibration perturbations must also change inferred estimates, while one contaminated standard must not overwhelm inference. No fixed answer, pooled fraction, expected truth table, or data-independent range meets the scientific objective. Numeric approximation tolerances are based on the variability of correct independent deconvolution methods; grading does not demand one solver's exact coefficients.
