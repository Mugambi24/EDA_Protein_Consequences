Batch-aware EDA-A1/A2 RNA deconvolution protocol (synthetic validation assay)

Scientific question: Do case/control RNA isoform fractions differ after adjusting for library-specific junction-capture bias and replicate uncertainty? The batch assay is simulated, is not patient RNA, and does not support clinical diagnoses. Each batch has a different and potentially nonlinear detection response; one middle-fraction standard replicate may be contaminated. Robust estimation from repeated standards is required, but the numerical regression or interpolation algorithm is your choice. The shared class is ambiguous RNA support, never a uniquely assigned A1/A2 molecule. Calibration must be learned separately for each batch, not pooled as a single conversion of raw A1-unique read counts to isoform fraction. An honest uncertainty interval should reflect both read sampling and between-batch disagreement.

Input `/data/batch_assay.json` has exactly `assay_id` (string), `synthetic` (true), `calibrators` (array), and `measurements` (array). Calibrators have exactly `batch_id` (string), `standard_id` (string), `known_a1_fraction` (0-1 number), `replicate` (integer), and `counts`. Measurements have exactly `case_id` (string), `group` ('case' or 'control'), `batch_id` (string), `replicate` (integer), and `counts`. Every `counts` object contains exactly three nonnegative integer fields `A1_unique`, `A2_unique`, and `shared`.

Require the same case IDs in batch measurements as in DNA and basic RNA observations. Batch calibration is informative only with >=4 distinct known fractions spanning >=0.8, >=2 standard replicates per fraction with at least 100 A1_unique+A2_unique reads in each retained replicate, an observed extreme-standard median A1-unique share difference >=0.25 after monotone calibration, and >=120 informative unique reads across measurement replicates per batch and panel. Use every batch that passes these calibration and read-count criteria; `informative_molecules` is the sum of A1_unique and A2_unique in all retained measurement replicates. At least two independent informative batches are needed for a panel estimate; two valid batches are sufficient, and there is no minimum of three or fixed total-molecule cutoff beyond the per-batch condition. Otherwise report an unidentifiable estimate instead of guessing. The observed DNA, basic RNA and batch-assay case-ID sets must match exactly. Reassigning A2-unique evidence to A1-unique at fixed total/calibration must increase the corresponding inferred A1 fraction; there is no extra undisclosed jump-size cutoff. Out-of-order input records must not change the result.

The output `/app/output/batch_inference.json` contains exactly `assay_id` and `records`. Records are sorted lexicographically and unique by `case_id`; each has exactly `case_id`, `case`, `control`, `delta_a1_fraction`, `shift_class`. Each of the case and control panels has exactly `a1_fraction`, `interval_95`, `informative_molecules`, `batches_used`, `quality_flag`. The last two counts are nonnegative JSON integers. For identifiable panels, `quality_flag='estimated'`; `a1_fraction` is finite, in [0,1], and rounded to at most six decimals; `interval_95` is a two-element ordered interval in [0,1] containing the estimate, with at most six decimal places per bound. For nonidentifiable panels, `quality_flag='unidentifiable'`, fraction and interval are null, and `batches_used` is less than two. `informative_molecules` counts unique A1/A2 evidence used by the retained batches (shared reads do not contribute). Delta is the six-decimal case-minus-control fraction computed from already rounded estimates, null when either estimate is unavailable. `shift_class` is `unidentifiable` for null delta, `A1_shift` for delta >=0.12, `A2_shift` for delta <=-0.12, or `no_clear_shift` otherwise. Use finite numerics, exact keys/types, no duplicate JSON keys.

The synthetic assay has independently generated known mixture fractions. In well-powered data, scientifically acceptable reconstruction should have mean absolute A1-fraction error <=0.035 and worst error <0.065, with at least 85% unexpanded interval coverage (counting fractions directly inside the published bounds and rounding the required covered-panel count up to the next whole panel), a mean interval width <0.32 and maximum width <0.45. Panel-level requirements apply to each case and control. Near-balanced measurements do not eliminate the need for batch calibration: the inferred values must respond to changes in observed isoform-specific RNA molecules rather than remain fixed. When selected measurements are perturbed, the changed panels must respond in the expected direction. Unmodified panels must retain scientifically accurate point estimates and valid uncertainty intervals; their entire JSON records and intervals need not remain byte-identical, because pooled dispersion or hierarchical uncertainty can depend on all assay panels. The same per-panel worst-error and interval-schema bounds continue to apply to unmodified panels after perturbation. No particular fitting formula is required. These are scientific performance requirements, not an invitation to encode the expected values. The inference must respond to changes in the standards, tolerate an isolated contaminated standard, and reject missing/extra sample IDs.

Each withheld case must respond to an RNA evidence counterfactual: for all case-group assay libraries, move min(A2_unique, round(0.30*(A1_unique+A2_unique))) molecules from A2_unique to A1_unique without changing molecule totals; the inferred case A1 fraction must increase by more than 0.02. The control panel is not changed. This is a method-response check alongside the separate truth-based accuracy and interval gates.
